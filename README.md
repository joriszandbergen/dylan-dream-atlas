# A field of connections — exploratory prototype, revision 17

A full-screen, white, generative field of Dylan van Dael's projects, with a subtle light gray square grid rotated 45° behind the drawing. The grid follows 86% of the map's movement for subtle parallax and is included in image exports. Reduced-motion preferences keep the grid and nodes moving together. Vivid red, cobalt blue, green, yellow and black ink strokes connect small ink marks and solid circular project markers in the four chromatic colors (never the black ink color), with lowercase labels that appear on hover or keyboard focus and a collapsible tuning panel. It is an independent concept, not an official portfolio.

Run `npm run dev` and open http://127.0.0.1:4173. `npm run check` verifies JavaScript syntax.

## Explore

Drag or use WASD / arrow keys to pan; scroll or use a two-finger gesture to pan. The drawing scale is fixed; there are no zoom, fit, or teleport controls. The field generates adjoining sectors in any direction. Project markers are simple solid coloured circles, slightly larger than the decorative dots. They gently enlarge on hover or keyboard focus, with no outlines or inset cutouts. Hover to reveal their labels, or use Tab to focus and Enter to open. On touch, tap directly to open. Select a marker or a project in the tuning panel to open an image aperture within the field, with its description, source and a connection to another project. Selection does not move the map. Colored paths show shared media relationships. Projects without a sourced image use an abstract contour illustration.

Every unseeded visit generates a fresh layout. **Keep this place** writes the seed, location and all plot settings to a reproducible URL and the clipboard. **Open world** reopens a known seed. **New field** creates another arrangement. Settings live in memory unless included in a copied URL. Export PNG saves the visible diagram at device resolution.

The tuning panel changes routing (flowing by default, also diagonal or orthogonal), spacing, line weight, label size, typography, path colors, drawing time, ink variation, notes, and optional flow. Flow is off by default and indicates paths, not actual audio or live data.

## Listen

Sound begins only after pressing **sound off** in the header, **enter with sound** during entry, or a preview button in the sound panel. The four recordings are downloaded from this private site and decoded once, on demand; loading failures leave sound off and allow retry. Enabling sound replays the visible drawing at the same position and seed. **The sliders beside sound** open a compact floating instrument. The **notes** tab controls root, A4 reference pitch, chord pace, note length, pitch glide, melody level/activity, chord layer and master volume. The **effects** tab controls reverb amount/decay, stereo delay amount/time/feedback, chorus amount/speed, saturation and tone. Close, drift and echo presets provide starting points; **compare dry** bypasses the entire effects rack without changing its settings. **Hear composition** auditions one complete cycle without moving the map; **replay the drawing** plays it with the generative animation. The highlighted chord shows the current harmonic position, and the phrase counter and chord names update as new routes emerge. Repeated previews advance to the next phrase; replaying the drawing restarts the seed’s sequence. Reset restores D Dorian, A4 = 440 Hz. Sound does not auto-start on saved links; all sound settings are included in saved plot settings.

`dist/sound.js` turns all four user-supplied recordings into a Web Audio sampler. Audio 2 supplies sustained chords and lines, Audio 4 supplies bass pulses, Audio 9 carries the melody and some lines, and Audio 19 supplies upper accents. Nodes and small marks also vary between the recordings. Every audible pitched layer uses a recording. The source selector chooses which original to compare. **Listen source** plays that complete, untransposed source without the map’s filtering or reverb, pausing generated gestures during that comparison. Growing line tips have continuous tones: height and thickness choose notes from the current chord, curves change timbre, and horizontal position sets stereo placement. Pitch targets stay within a resolving Dorian progression generated from the field seed and phrase number. Each four-chord phrase departs from the tonic, chooses a weighted Dorian cadence and returns home. Minor ninth and minor 6/9 tonic colors vary; voice leading keeps adjacent chord tones close. Successive phrases change the departure, and the natural sixth remains part of each route. Optional glide smooths the transitions between tuned notes. Root selection transposes the entire progression. A quiet five-voice chord layer supports the generated gestures, and phrases return to the tonic before settling into silence. `dist/harmony.js` centralizes chord voicings, note quantization, transposition, reference pitch and input validation. Marks make short notes as the drawing tip crosses them. Circular project markers play the recording’s attack and decay at project-specific pitches. Small marks use short fragments from its body. Project arrival, hover/focus and selection play the appropriate shape voice. Optional moving flow dots also sound; settled, inactive geometry is quiet.

Only visible drawing motion plays. The mix is limited to eight continuous voices and 24 short sound gestures, plus a five-voice chord layer, with bounded event history, a compressor and a soft limiter. A synthesized stereo impulse provides adjustable convolution reverb; impulse changes are debounced and crossfaded. Stereo echoes use damped feedback capped at 68%; chorus uses two short modulated delays, and saturation blends a soft waveshaper with the clean signal. Reverb and delay buffers are cleared on mute, reset, retuning and bypass changes. Modulation oscillators are only connected to delay parameters, never to the audio output. Mute stops voices and suspends audio; hidden tabs suspend it too. Audio processing happens locally. The only audio downloads are the eight prepared sample/sustain assets hosted with this site; there is no microphone access or third-party audio service. These supplied recordings are not labelled as music from any of the portfolio projects. Reduced-motion mode retains interaction sounds without adding animation.

Run `npm test` for sound lifecycle, event deduplication, geometry-to-sound mapping, polyphony limits, Dorian chord membership across roots, reference tuning, live retuning, progression timing, tonic resolution and effect cleanup, sample-only playback, one-time loading, retry after failure, cancellation during loading and original-source audition, effect limits/bypass/cleanup, melodic scale membership, seed reproducibility, tonic endings and non-self-sustaining composition scheduling. Browser verification measured nonzero output during drawing, silence after settling and muting, and checked replay, volume and mobile controls.

### Composition and source preparation

`dist/melody.js` produces a recurring eight-subdivision motif for each chord, with seed-dependent contours, rests and Dorian passing notes. Strong subdivisions follow the currently generated chord, including when the harmonic route changes. The final note returns to the root. Audio 9 carries the motif; Audio 19 answers with accents. Audio 4 articulates each chord's bass root. Notes use the audio clock, skip missed subdivisions rather than catching up in a burst, and cannot keep an idle field playing indefinitely. Melody level and activity can be set to zero independently of the chords. **Hear composition** previews one full cycle.

The supplied files are **Audio 2** (2.5 s), **Audio 4** (0.60 s), **Audio 9** (1.13 s), and **Audio 19** (1.0 s), all 24-bit PCM at 44.1 kHz. `python3 scripts/prepare-audio.py /absolute/path/to/source-folder` prepares all four named originals using ffmpeg and NumPy. It removes DC offset, leaves headroom, adds 5 ms boundary fades and writes 16-bit stereo PCM plus period-aligned, crossfaded sustain loops into `dist/assets/audio/`. It also writes source metadata and `dist/audio-source.js`. The original files are never modified.

Measured pitch centres are approximately 350.50, 58.48, 197.56 and 392.53 Hz respectively; the last is the dominant upper partial of the accent sound. Playback-rate transposition maps each recording to the selected tuning while retaining its internal character. Pitch shifting also changes playback speed. The preparation windows are specific to these recordings and should be adapted for different source material.

## Generation

`dist/world.js` implements a deterministic hash and random stream per seed and sector. Ports are hashed from shared boundary coordinates, so neighboring sectors agree on their connection points. Only visible sectors are rendered; offscreen cached sectors are discarded after the cache reaches its limit. There is no enclosing map boundary. The **archive remains finite**: the seven documented projects and fragments recur in new arrangements throughout the field.

`dist/app.js` handles canvas rendering, panning, touch translation, selection, URL serialization, and the tuning panel. `dist/projects.js` holds sourced content. `dist/ink.js` samples seeded curves and constructs variable-width ink strokes, with rounded thick sections and small score-like marks. Every complete connection has one drawing timeline and one moving tip; marks appear only after that tip reaches them. No path fragments, blur, haze or opacity reveal are used. Direction is chosen when the complete stroke first enters the viewport, and its progress stays continuous while panning. Nodes emerge as their lines reach them. A radial entry sequence introduces the field and can be skipped immediately. Hovering gently emphasizes connections, project apertures unfold from their nodes, and pointer drags ease out with light momentum. Reduced-motion preferences disable entry, stroke drawing, reveal transitions and momentum. No WebGL, framework, build step, or server state is required.

## Design question

Can the act of following an emerging connection become an exploratory interface to creative work? The prototype asks this through procedural curves, spatial relationships and in-field project reveals. Awaiting exploration and feedback; retain the validated direction and discard or rewrite the prototype implementation when moving to production.
